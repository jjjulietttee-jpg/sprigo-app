How to Measure Light for Your Houseplants With an iPhone

Updated October 2026 · By the Sprigo developer

Short answer. Hold your iPhone where the plant sits, with the back camera facing the light, and a light meter app reads the exposure to estimate brightness. Sprigo's light meter turns that into a lux number and a plain label: low light, indirect light, bright window or direct sun. It is an estimate, enough to tell a shady corner from a sunny sill.

Step by step

  1. Open the light meterChoose the Light tool in Sprigo and allow camera access. Frames are not saved.
  2. Hold the phone where the plant sitsFace the back camera the way the leaves face the window and keep it steady for a few seconds.
  3. Read the labelUnder about 800 lux is low light, up to 2,500 is indirect, up to 10,000 is a bright window, and above that is direct sun.
  4. Use it for the plantChoose that light level when adding the plant. Sprigo uses it, with pot and season, to set the watering interval.

Frequently asked questions

How does the camera measure light?

It uses the camera's automatic exposure (aperture, shutter time and ISO) to estimate lux, and takes a median of several readings.

Is it as accurate as a lux meter?

No. It gives the right order of magnitude, which is enough to tell a shady corner from a sunny sill, but it is not a calibrated instrument.

Does it save photos?

No. The meter only reads exposure. The separate photo journal stores plant photos inside the app, not in your photo library.

Which light does each label mean?

Low light, indirect light, bright window and direct sun, split at roughly 800, 2,500 and 10,000 lux.

Why does light matter for watering?

Plants in brighter light use water faster. Sprigo shortens the interval for direct sun and lengthens it for low light.

Does it need the internet?

No. The measurement happens on the phone.

Can it tell me which plant fits a spot?

It tells you how bright the spot is and each plant in the catalog lists the light it prefers. It does not identify plants.
